description: Why a variable is named as its context plus the type it holds, and why participles and vague words are refused.
---

**Why:** the type name is what a reader looks up, and the context is what tells
two values of one type apart. A situational name such as `done`, `result` or
`it`, or a participle such as `named` or `mentioned`, says how the value was
reached rather than what it is, and sends the reader back to the line that set
it. On 2026-10-02 the user found `named`, `mentioned` and `cited` hard to read
in `CharterRoot`, though the earlier version of this guide asked for type names:
it said nothing about participles or about adding context.

**How to apply:** take the type's noun in camelCase as the last word; put the
role the value plays in this function in front of it when the type alone is
ambiguous; make a loop variable singular; prefix a boolean with `is` or `has`.
